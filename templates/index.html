Data Sources, Methodology & Limitations

Documented scientific provenance, astronomical formulas, and physical limitations.

Authoritative NASA / JPL Sources

1. Small-Body Database (SBDB) Query API
ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/sbdb_query.api

Provides high-precision osculating Keplerian orbital elements (a, e, i, Ω, ω, M, epoch), physical properties (H, diameter, albedo), and official PHA classifications for all cataloged small bodies.

2. Sentry Earth Impact Monitoring System
ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/sentry.api

Automated collision monitoring system continuously scanning potential asteroid impact solutions over the next 100+ years, reporting Palermo Technical Scale, Torino Scale, and cumulative impact probabilities.

3. Close Approach Data (CAD) API
ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/cad.api

Provides verified flyby encounters to Earth, reporting calendar date/time, nominal miss distance (AU and Lunar Distances), and relative velocity (km/s).

Physical & Astronomical Formulations

1. NASA Photometric Diameter Estimation:
For asteroids lacking direct thermal infrared (NEOWISE) or radar imaging, diameter is calculated from absolute magnitude H and geometric albedo pv (default 0.14):
D = (1329 / √pv) × 10-0.2 H [km]
2. Öpik-Tisserand Encounter Velocity:
Encounter velocity relative to Earth is approximated using Öpik's formula in units of Earth's orbital velocity (vE ≈ 29.78 km/s):
vrel = √(3 - 1/a - 2√(a(1-e2))cos(i)) × 29.78 km/s
3. Impact Kinetic Energy:
Estimated assuming mean spherical volume, typical S-type/C-type asteroid density ρ = 2,500 kg/m3, and encounter velocity v = 20 km/s:
Ek = 0.5 × M × v2,   (1 Megaton TNT = 4.184 × 1015 J)

Scientific Limitations & Ethical Distinctions

  • Model Classification vs. Definitive Impact Forecasts: Our machine learning models classify an asteroid's geometric hazard potential based on orbital intersection distance (MOID) and physical size (H/diameter). A high model hazard probability does not mean an immediate impact will occur tomorrow; it identifies objects requiring rigorous astrometric follow-up.
  • Authoritative Sentry Status: Official Earth impact risk is calculated exclusively through NASA JPL Sentry and ESA NEODyS, which perform full N-body numerical integration with orbit covariance error ellipsoids and non-gravitational Yarkovsky forces.
  • 2-Body Keplerian Orbit Visualizer: The 3D trajectory viewer propagates elliptical 2-body Keplerian orbits to provide intuitive spatial awareness. Real orbital solutions experience gravitational perturbations from Jupiter and the Sun, causing nodal precession over century timescales.
